Getting up to speed with AI

Welcome. Today covers why we use AI, what to get in place first, how to prompt well, what to watch out for, and then everyone builds something.

I · First decision

Why AI?

Airliner: do less

"Draft my weekly update for the team from my task list."

Fighter jet: do more

"Read every conversation with our families this quarter and find the three patterns I'd never have time to find."

Airliner: autopilot takes work away from the pilot. It does more of my job so I can do other things. Fighter jet: the technology asks more of the pilot, who uses it to push the limits. More, better, faster. As the technology has advanced, the expectations of the fighter pilot have gone up, not down. Both are valid reasons to use AI. Ask each person: which one are you here for? Then make it clear: this session is about the fighter jet. It is about doing more.

It's not the plane, it's the pilot.

Top Gun: Maverick

Now that we have chosen the fighter jet: doing more depends on the pilot, not the plane. AI is the plane. You are the pilot. What you get out of it depends on you. We come back to this at the warnings and at the end.

II · Baseline

AI can't accelerate what it can't access.

It can't access

  • Handwritten notes
  • What's only in your head
  • Things scattered across different places

It can access

  • Typed notes in one home
  • One task list
  • Standards that are written down
The whole idea: AI can't access what isn't written somewhere it can reach. It can't accelerate or support what it can't access. Handwritten notes, or things scattered across different places, don't give it a clear, actionable signal. Ask the room: where does your work live that AI could never see?

V · Prompt quality

Better prompts, better results.

Weak

"Write an email to a family about the delay."

Strong

"Write to [name], who is organizing support for the [family] family. Their payout is two days late because of a bank check. They are already stressed. Own it, give the new date, offer a call. Under 100 words, in my voice."

The weaker the prompt, the more common the reply. The weak prompt gets a reply that sounds like everyone. The strong prompt gets a reply that sounds like you. A strong prompt says who it is for, what happened, how they feel, what you want, and the limits. Run both live and compare. Replace the bracketed names with a real family, and swap the payout delay for a situation the team sees often.

VI · Day to day

Expand, collapse, repeat.

One goal

A reply to a family

A proposal for a partner

An update for the team

A data pull

One result

More sessions take more focus, not less.

Before: wait inside one session until the work is done. Now: expand across several sessions, then collapse them back down as the work is merged or combined. This is why AI takes more attention than ever: you are managing many sessions and each one has to keep progressing toward its desired outcome. Example to share: this deck was built in one session while a second session built the activity page, and the two were combined at the end.

VII · Warning

Slop bombing

"Don't share anything you haven't read, reviewed... and highlighted."

Slop bombing is dropping unread AI output on someone else and making it their job to sort out. Bad example: forwarding an AI summary you never read, with a wrong number in it. Done right: the same summary, read and corrected, with two lines highlighted and a note on why they matter. The highlight is the proof you read it.

VII · Warning

AI has nothing to lose. You do.

Credibility.
Trust.

"The dumbest person you know is currently being told 'You're absolutely right!' by an AI chatbot."

Back to the pilot. The plane has nothing at stake. The pilot does. And a pilot who leans on autopilot for everything stops being able to fly. AI will agree with almost anything and it carries none of the consequences. Your name is on the work. Agreement from a chatbot is not evidence that you are right.

VIII · Routines

Turn a good prompt into a routine

Every Monday

"Check last week's email. Find the families, teammates, and partners I owe a reply. Draft each reply."

Every Friday

"Summarize this week's meeting notes. List what I promised to families, teammates, and partners."

The Monday routine is the first test prompt on a schedule: once a week, look back one week, and draft the replies. The routine drafts. You still read, review, and send. Anything a routine drafts goes through the slop bombing rule before it leaves your hands.

X · Before you go

If you aren't hitting limits, you aren't hitting hard enough.

Most platforms have usage limits and restrictions at certain volumes. Never hit a limit? You aren't using the tools enough, so use them more. Hitting them every week? That is the signal to upgrade. Ask the room: who has hit a limit this month?
